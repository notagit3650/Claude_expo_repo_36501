Even a fact read once leaves a trace. A weak one gets overruled by the layers that write the answer.

Here's the hunch behind Way one. How well a model answers depends on how often it has read a fact: repeated facts, easy. But even a fact read once leaves a small, real trace. The information is there, weakly. What we know from interpretability work, Geva and colleagues 2023, is that the early layers look the facts up and the later layers assemble the answer. A weak fact gets overruled on the way out by everything else. So if the knowledge is in there, we don't need to train. We need to stop it getting lost.
